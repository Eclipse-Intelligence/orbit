## What Mark asked for

Mark sends more and more email to prospects through Orbit, and much of it is cold. Sending cold email from his everyday Microsoft 365 addresses puts his main domains at risk, and a campaign of thousands from those mailboxes would mostly fail.

He will buy lookalike domains (for x.com: getx.com, tryx.com, usex.com), set up mailboxes on them in Smartlead and warm them up. Normal mail and replies keep going from his normal addresses. Cold outreach goes through Smartlead campaigns, run entirely from Orbit:

- **Prospecting is a daily workflow.** Every weekday the agent finds prospects that meet his criteria (for example 20 a day), adds anyone new to Orbit, tags the run (for example "Agency run 16 Nov"), enriches them, checks the email is real, researches them, and adds them to a Smartlead campaign. The campaign grows every day.
- **Workflows run items side by side.** Hundreds of prospects are researched and enriched at once, not queued one after another.
- **Variables make every email unique.** The email skeleton lives in Smartlead with variables (`{{opener}}`, `{{news_hook}}`, ...). Orbit knows which variables each campaign needs and what each should be about, researches the prospect (their business, competitors, blog, news, content), mixes that with what Eclipse does and how it talks, fills every variable and pushes the prospect into the campaign.
- **Everything comes back into Orbit.** Campaigns, their status, all stats, per-step performance, every prospect, every email sent and every reply. Mark or a colleague answers replies from Orbit and never needs to open Smartlead (LinkedIn replies come only from the sender's own member, Part 9).
- **Each workspace has its own campaigns.**
- **The assistant and MCP** can do the same: "add these 50 to the agency campaign", "how did email 2 do this week?".
- **LinkedIn too** (added 2026-11-16): LinkedIn outreach through HeyReach, mixed with email in the same workflows, as simple as possible. See Part 9.

## Decisions settled with Mark (2026-11-16)

1. **Smartlead** is the sending platform. Orbit does no cold sending of its own and adds no cold addresses to the From menu.
2. **Workspace = Smartlead client.** Each workspace stores its own Smartlead client API key, entered by an owner or admin in Settings › Tools › Smartlead and encrypted with `APP_SECRET` (`encryptSecret`, `lib/crypto/secret.ts:8`). Smartlead then enforces the boundary, and each workspace gets its own rate budget (Smartlead's plan limits are per key; its per-endpoint limits are per account, Part 1).
3. **Campaigns are shared with the workspace,** like companies and contacts: every member sees campaigns, prospects, threads and replies, and any member can answer by email (LinkedIn replies come only from the sender's own member, Part 9). This differs from Mail, which stays personal. Campaign replies never enter the personal Mail mirror.
4. **Sequences are read-only in Orbit in v1.** Mark writes the email steps in Smartlead; Orbit reads them, lists their variables and shows them. Pause and resume work from Orbit.
5. **Unsubscribes and bounces build a workspace suppression list.** It blocks campaigns, bulk email, workflow Send email, the assistant's `send_email` and MCP `mail_send`, and only warns in the one-to-one composer.
6. **Do-not-contact rule** before any prospect goes into a campaign: skip customers, people on open opportunities, anyone the workspace has written to or met, and suppressed people, addresses and LinkedIn profiles. Each skip records its reason, shown on the run.
7. **An email check of "unknown"** (accept-all domains, and anything else the checker can't decide: the code has one `unknown` verdict for all of them, `lib/enrichment/providers.ts:29`) is skipped and counted, like "invalid".
8. **One-off cold emails** go through a campaign whose step is just `{{subject}}` and `{{body}}`. The composer and From menu don't change.
9. **Reply notifications** go to the person whose workflow or action added the prospect, otherwise to the workspace owners. Any member can answer by email.
10. **Smartlead's global unsubscribe and domain block** cover the whole Smartlead account, so both workspaces. Accepted for opt-outs; tested before relying on it.
11. **PECR:** prospects are company addresses only (no sole traders or partnerships). Every template carries the sender's identity and an opt-out line. The prospecting criteria say so.
12. **Smartlead leads are called "prospects"** in Orbit, because "lead" already means Meta leads and the `leads:write` scope.
13. **Variable filling guards only against invention.** No approval queue. The existing Approval step and test runs cover anyone who wants to read the first few before they go.

## The shape

```
Daily workflow (schedule, weekdays)
  Ask AI (web) ── finds 20 to 200 prospects that fit
  For each item  ── runs in parallel ──────────────────────────────┐
    Find or create company and contact, tag "Agency run 16 Nov"    │ one child run
    Find and check email (enrich + check), skip if not valid       │ per item,
    Ask AI (web): research news, competitors, blog, content        │ hundreds
    Add to campaign: do-not-contact check, fill variables, push    │ at once
  Done ── summary: 187 added, 9 skipped (reasons), 4 failed ───────┘

Smartlead ── email: sends from the warmed mailboxes, rotates, follows up
HeyReach ─── LinkedIn: invites, messages, InMail from each sender's account
   │ webhooks (hints) + polling (truth)
Orbit ── Campaigns page, prospects, threads, replies, stats,
         timeline, notifications, suppression, workflow triggers
```

## Part 1: Parallel workflows

The engine change comes first and helps every workflow, not only campaigns.

### Parallel For each item

- For each item gets **Run items at the same time** (on by default for new loops, off for existing ones so nothing changes under them) and **At most N at once** (default 50, up to 500). A parallel loop takes up to 1,000 items; a loop that runs one at a time keeps today's limit (default 25, at most 100, `engine.ts:1055`).
- When a parallel loop starts, the engine creates one **child run** per item in `crm.workflow_runs`, with new columns `parent_run_id` (references `crm.workflow_runs`, `on delete cascade`), `loop_step_id` and `item_index`. A child copies the parent's `workspace_id`, `workflow_id`, `owner_id`, `definition`, `test` and `depth` (the table requires `workflow_id` and `definition`), and starts with `state.vars` holding its variables and `state.at` holding the node on the loop's `each` edge. Unique on `(parent_run_id, loop_step_id, item_index)`, so a reclaimed parent never creates an item twice. Children are created in batches of 500 rows.
- A child runs the loop body only. It ends where the body path ends, exactly as one iteration does today (with no loop frame open, `nextLoopItem` returns nothing and the run finishes, `engine.ts:473`). Its variables are the parent's variables at loop start plus its item: `vars.loop` (`item`, `index`, `total`) is set as `loopVars` sets it (`engine.ts:455`), and a record item is loaded as the run's company and contact as `applyLoopRecord` does (`engine.ts:433`). Changes it makes to run variables stay in the child. Code that tests for an open loop frame, such as the AI skip rule (`engine.ts:1485`), treats a child as inside a loop.
- The parent waits: `status = 'waiting'` with `waiting_for = 'children'` (added to the `waiting_for` check, which allows only `delay` and `approval` today) and `state.waitingChildren`. Each child that finishes updates new counter columns on the parent in the same transaction as its own final status (`children_succeeded`, `children_skipped`, `children_failed`). The last one queues the parent, which carries on from the loop's `done` edge. The counts and the list of skip reasons are the loop step's output, beside today's `count`: `{{steps.<loop id>.succeeded}}`, `{{steps.<loop id>.skipped}}`, `{{steps.<loop id>.failed}}` and `{{steps.<loop id>.skipReasons}}`. They can't sit under `{{loop.*}}`, which the engine removes, or hands back to an outer loop, when a loop ends (`engine.ts:482`).
- **Rules at save time** (`graphProblem`, `lib/workflows/definition.ts:221`, which every save goes through in `definitionFromInput`): no Approval step inside a parallel loop; a parallel loop can't contain another parallel loop (inner loops in a child run one at a time as today). The editor explains why in the step's error line.
- **Failures:** a failed child doesn't fail the parent. An AI step that still fails after its retry skips the item, as loops do today (`engine.ts:1485`). Run statuses have no `skipped`, so a skipped child finishes as `succeeded` with its reason in a new `skip_reason` column, and counts in `children_skipped`. The parent's run note lists skips and failures, and one notification summarises the run (today's `finish` already sends one when items were skipped, `engine.ts:1412`).
- **Cancelling:** whenever a parent ends as `cancelled`, its queued and waiting children are cancelled with it, and deleting a parent deletes its children (`on delete cascade`). Orbit has no Cancel run action today (see What Orbit has today), and none is listed in the phases below.
- **Test runs** run the first three items in parallel and describe the rest. A test run executes inline in `startRun` (`store.ts:386`), so its three children run in that same call, with side effects described and not carried out, as test runs do today (`engine.ts:1040`).
- **Privacy:** children belong to the parent's owner and workspace and follow the same run policies (owner-only, as above).

### Workers that run many runs at once

- `runWorkflowTick` runs claimed runs **concurrently** with a worker pool (up to 25 runs at once per invocation) instead of `for ... await` (`engine.ts:1597`). Model, web and enrichment calls hold no database connection, so 25 runs share the two pooled connections. Every step is checked so that no `withActor` or `appTransaction` stays open across a call to an outside service. A test runs 25 items concurrently under `useServerlessPool()`. That helper only catches nested connections, so the test also checks that no call fails waiting for a connection (the pool gives up after 5 seconds, `lib/db/pool.ts:31`).
- **Fan out:** when the queue is deeper than one invocation can take, `/api/cron/workflows` (and the tick after an event or a parallel loop starts) starts more invocations of a new route, `/api/workflows/worker` (`app/api/workflows/` doesn't exist yet; authorised with `CRON_SECRET` through `cronAuthorization`, `maxDuration` 300), up to **10 workers at once**. That is up to 250 runs in flight. The number of live workers is kept with a lease row per worker in a new table, `crm.workflow_workers` (expires after 5 minutes; taken and released through `private.*` functions, as `private.ai_field_run_lease` does for AI fields, `20261231100000_ai_fields.sql:353`), so a burst of triggers never starts more than the limit.
- **Every worker is a long tick,** so a web-search AI step no longer waits for the `/api/cron/workflows` job (every two minutes, about 280 seconds each). Page-load and mail ticks stay short and leave web steps to workers.
- Workers claim with `private.claim_workflow_runs(p_limit, p_long)` and a limit of 25. The function already allows up to 50 and uses `for update skip locked`. Any change to it starts from its latest definition, `20270205100000_workflow_long_steps.sql`. It never claims a waiting parent, because it takes `waiting` rows only when `waiting_for = 'delay'`. The older one-argument version (`20261104100000_workflows.sql:286`) is unused and is left alone.

### Shared limits, so parallel runs wait instead of failing

- A small limiter, `lib/workflows/limits.ts`, built on `private.consume_rate_limit` (latest definitions `20261013110000_link_checks_and_guards.sql:330` and, with a cost, `20270111400000_review_batch_3.sql:303`; granted to `crm_app`, so called with `query()`) and a new matching pair, `private.take_slot` and `private.release_slot`, for things capped by how many run at once (nothing like them exists yet). One entry per outside service: the LLM gateway, web search, context.dev, Mindcase, the email checkers (Enrow, Icypeas, Hunter) and Smartlead. Smartlead's documented limits (https://api.smartlead.ai/guides/rate-limits) are 60 a minute and 1,000 an hour per key on the Standard plan (120 and 3,000 on Pro), and 10 a minute on `leads-statistics`, counted per account across all campaigns, so both workspaces share that one bucket. Values come from environment variables with safe defaults.
- When a service is busy or returns 429, the step **waits and re-queues** (`resume_at`, like Delay) instead of failing. `private.consume_rate_limit` uses fixed windows and counts refused calls too, so a refused step waits until its window ends before asking again. Smartlead's ordinary 429s carry `Retry-After`, which is followed; its per-endpoint 429s (`leads-statistics`) carry none, so those wait up to 60 seconds (same page).
- **Enrichment limit:** `enrichmentAllowed`'s 300 an hour per person is for people clicking in the app. Workflow runs use the service limiters above instead, so a 200-prospect run isn't stopped at the 300th lookup. `enrichContact` doesn't call `enrichmentAllowed` (its callers do), so a step calls it as it is. `verifyEmail` calls it inside (`lib/enrichment/email-check.ts:137`), so it gains an option that skips that check, passed only by workflow steps and fill jobs.

### Fixes that come with it

- **Step limit:** a run that reaches `MAX_STEPS_PER_RUN` saves its place and re-queues instead of finishing as succeeded. A run that goes past 5,000 steps in total fails with "This workflow ran more than 5,000 steps. Check it doesn't loop."
- `find_records` takes up to 1,000 records when feeding a parallel loop (100 otherwise). The limit is read in `readFindConfig` (`lib/workflows/records.ts:56`).
- **Runs page:** a parent run shows progress ("143 of 200 done, 4 skipped, 1 failed"), and its children are listed under it with their own steps. Skipped items show their reason.

### New workflow steps

- **Find or create** gets **Tags**: tags added to the record it finds or creates (variables allowed, for example `Agency run {{today}}`). `update_record` gets tags too (add or remove). Both go through `addTags` and `removeTags` (`lib/crm/tags.ts:51`), which keep the limits of 20 tags a record and 40 characters a tag (`lib/crm/normalize.ts:213`).
- **Find and check email:** finds the contact's email if it's missing (`enrichContact` with `email`), checks it (`verifyContactEmail`), and ends the item's path with a skip reason when no email is found (including when `enrichContact` returns `skipped` for a missing name or company website) or the check is invalid or unknown. Its output is `status` and `address`, read as `{{steps.<step id>.status}}` and `{{steps.<step id>.address}}`. `{{email.*}}` already belongs to the Email received trigger (`lib/workflows/variables.ts:176`).
- **Add to campaign** (Part 4).

## Part 3: Data model

All tables are workspace-shared with row-level security enabled and forced, read by every member, written through `lib/crm/campaigns.ts` and `private.*` functions for sync. Each has `id`, `workspace_id` and `created_at` besides the columns listed. Workspace agent tokens run as `crm_agent`, so each table also gets `crm_agent` policies on `private.request_workspace_id()`, as `crm.enrichment_lookups` has (`20261031100000_enrichment.sql:80`).

Sync has no signed-in person. Its writes to these tables go through `private.*` functions called with `query()`. ## Part 4: Add to campaign and filling variables

- One function, `addProspects(actor, campaignId, contacts, provenance)`, is used by every route: the workflow step, the bulk bar, the assistant, MCP and the REST API. It runs the do-not-contact check, then writes `campaign_prospects` rows as `queued` (or `skipped` with a reason). Adding the same person twice to one campaign does nothing. `actor` is a person or an agent token, and `addProspects` asserts `campaigns:write` with `assertScope`, as other `lib/crm` writes do.
- **Do-not-contact check:** suppressed address; company is a customer (`crm.companies.lifecycle` is free text, so this is `lower(lifecycle) = 'customer'`, the value Orbit sets when an opportunity is won, `lib/crm/opportunities.ts:300`); person or company on an open opportunity (the test `find_records` already uses, `lib/workflows/records.ts:74`: a row in `crm.opportunities` with `status = 'open'` and not archived, which counts board cards, since a card is an opportunity); anyone the workspace has written to or met (the Capture rule, across members' mailboxes through a new `private.*` function that answers yes or no only, so no private mail is exposed; it joins `crm.mail_known_addresses` to `crm.workspace_members`, and because that table is per person with no workspace, a member of both workspaces counts in both); already replied to any campaign in this workspace (matched like suppression: by contact, email or LinkedIn profile); already in another active campaign on the same channel. Email campaigns also skip an email check that isn't valid; LinkedIn campaigns skip "no LinkedIn profile" and "no first or last name". Each skip names its reason ("Skipped: open opportunity").
- **Filling** (`lib/campaigns/fill.ts`): for each queued prospect, one model call (`completeJson` in `lib/llm.ts`, with a zod schema and a deadline) with the campaign's steps, every variable and its brief, the contact and company, the run's variables (so research from earlier steps is used), the workspace playbook, outreach brief and Tone (`brandOrEmpty`) and proof list (`listProof`), and web research when a brief asks for it. It returns every variable with the sources it used. The cold email check's evidence rule applies: a figure, claim or proof must trace to a source or Orbit's own proof list, and a value that can't be supported is left empty. `checkColdEmail` judges a whole subject and body, so filling reuses the pieces behind its `numbers_sourced` and `proof_approved` checks (`figuresIn` and `withProofList` in `lib/assistant/cold-email.ts`) on each value. Any required variable left empty skips the prospect ("Skipped: nothing found for news_hook"). Values are capped at the variable's Max characters when set and at what the provider accepts (provider limits unconfirmed, test lists), and never contain `{{` or `{`. `brandOrEmpty`, `listProof`, `enrichContact` and `verifyContactEmail` all need a `UserActor`, so filling runs as the person who added the prospect; for a prospect added by an agent token it runs as the member sync acts as (Part 3).
- **Pushing:** before the POST the row is marked `pushing` (the ClickUp "unconfirmed" pattern: `createRemote` records what it is about to create and `findUnconfirmed` looks for it before creating again, `lib/crm/clickup.ts:651` and `:523`). The transaction that marks it `pushing` re-runs the suppression and already-replied checks first. On success it saves `external_lead_id` and becomes `pushed`. Smartlead's documented reply to adding leads gives counts and skipped leads, not ids (https://api.smartlead.ai/guides/lead-management), so the id comes from looking the lead up by email, or from the next poll (test list). A reclaimed `pushing` row looks the lead up by email in the campaign before pushing again. Pushes use Smartlead's duplicate controls (`ignore_duplicate_leads_in_other_campaign: false`, and `ignore_global_block_list` and `ignore_unsubscribe_list` left false so both lists are respected). Pushes batch up to 400 when many are ready together (the documented maximum per request).
- **In a workflow**, Add to campaign fills and pushes inline in the item's child run, so hundreds fill at once. **From the bulk bar, assistant, MCP or API**, prospects are queued and the workflow workers claim them as jobs, so the same parallel workers and limiters do the work. A job can't be a `crm.workflow_runs` row as that table stands (`workflow_id` and `definition` are required, and `private.claim_workflow_runs` returns only run, workspace and owner ids), so the job is the `queued` prospect row itself: a new `private.claim_campaign_fills(p_limit)` with `for update skip locked` takes rows by setting `claimed_at` (reclaimed after ten minutes, like runs), and each worker calls it in the same loop as the run claim, within the same 25 at once.
- **Sizing:** the campaign page shows sending capacity (mailboxes × their daily limit from Smartlead) against the sends the current prospects need over the next days, and warns when new prospects a day exceed what the mailboxes can carry with follow-ups ("10 mailboxes send 200 a day. At 4 steps, about 50 new prospects a day fit."). Reading mailbox limits with a client key is unconfirmed (test list).

The route has no member to act as, so it reads the connection's secret through a `private.*` function granted to `crm_app` (as `private.agent_mailbox_targets()` does, `20261226100000_agent_mailbox.sql:252`), decrypts and compares it in Node, and records the receipt through another.

## Part 9: LinkedIn through HeyReach

Added 2026-11-16. Mark wants LinkedIn outreach mixed with email in the same workflows, as simple as possible: clean, native and exceptional. The decisions below are settled with Mark (2026-11-16).

### Why HeyReach

HeyReach has a public API on every paid plan: campaigns (create, pause, resume), adding leads with custom variables, the inbox (read and send), lead tags, stats and webhooks, at 300 requests a minute. Growth is $79 per sender a month ($64 billed yearly); a sender is one LinkedIn account. HeyReach runs the sequences, daily limits and account safety; Orbit drives it. Drake has no API, Meet Alfred's API can't reply or send events, and account APIs such as Unipile would make Orbit build sequencing and pacing itself. These points come from the providers' sites, not from code; the HeyReach ones this plan builds on are on the test list below.

### The idea

- **One Campaigns area, two channels.** A campaign's channel is Email (Smartlead) or LinkedIn (HeyReach). Same list, same page, same prospects table, same Add to campaign step, same assistant tools. Only the stats and steps shown differ.
- **Orbit is the only place that mixes channels.** HeyReach's own Add to Smartlead step isn't used: two tools passing people between each other means two places to look when something goes wrong. Workflows do the mixing, with everything Orbit knows.
- **One person, one conversation.** A prospect's thread shows their emails and LinkedIn messages together in time order. The composer sends on the channel picked, starting on the one they last replied on.
- **A reply anywhere stops everything.** When a prospect replies on any channel, Orbit stops them in every other campaign in the workspace. Always on, no setting. Automatic and Out of office replies don't count.

### Decisions

1. **Workspace = HeyReach workspace.** Each Orbit workspace stores its own HeyReach API key in Settings › Tools › HeyReach (owners and admins), encrypted like the Smartlead key.
2. **Senders are people.** Each LinkedIn account in HeyReach is linked to the Orbit member it belongs to, matched on connect (the matching rule is pending with Mark). Only owners and admins change the link; each change is audited (`campaign.sender_linked`) and tells the member linked and the one unlinked. Removing a member from the workspace clears their links, in `private.team_remove_records` (latest definition `20270203100000_recurring_tasks.sql:874`). That function writes its audit rows and webhook deliveries in SQL, so the `campaign.sender_linked` rows for cleared links are written there the same way. The removed member isn't told, because they can no longer read that workspace's notifications. An unlinked account can't be replied from; its reply notifications go to whoever added the prospect, otherwise the owners.
3. **Only the sender replies on LinkedIn.** Every member sees LinkedIn campaign threads (campaigns are shared, decision 3), but a LinkedIn reply goes out as a real person, so only the member linked to that account can send it. Others see "Only Mark Ridgeon can reply from this LinkedIn account." `replyToProspect` enforces it for every route: the account is read from the conversation (not the prospect row), the person must still be a member, and agent tokens and workflows can't reply. With several conversations for one person, the reply goes to the one with the latest message from them. The approval card says "Send on LinkedIn as Mark Ridgeon".
4. **Only campaign conversations come in.** HeyReach's "track and import all conversations" stays off, and Orbit also enforces it itself: it stores a LinkedIn message only when its profile matches a pushed prospect in this workspace and its account is that prospect's `sender_id` (or, while `sender_id` is empty, one of the campaign's `sender_ids`, which then sets it), and only messages sent after `pushed_at`. Anything else is dropped, so earlier personal messages with a connection never come in.
5. **Sequences are written in HeyReach** and read-only in Orbit, like Smartlead.
6. **Variables work the same way:** briefs on the campaign, filled per prospect (Part 4), pushed as HeyReach custom variables. Names use letters, numbers and underscores only. A variable used in a connection note gets a Max characters (Part 3), so the note fits LinkedIn's limit. Filling follows the playbook's rule: a connection note is one plain line or nothing, with no pitch and no mention of the emails. That rule is one line of the default playbook (`COLD_EMAIL_PLAYBOOK`, `lib/assistant/cold-email.ts:76`) and is missing when a workspace has written its own, so the fill prompt states it itself.
7. **Do not contact is per person, across channels** (the suppression rule in Part 3). An email unsubscribe sets it automatically. LinkedIn has no unsubscribe event, so there a person sets Do not contact from the thread or contact page; a reply has already stopped everything and decision 9 keeps them out of later campaigns.
8. **At most one active campaign per channel per person.** Being in an email and a LinkedIn campaign at once is allowed; that is the point.
9. **Anyone who has replied to any campaign** in the workspace is skipped by Add to campaign ("Skipped: already replied"), so a delayed workflow path can't restart a conversation. It doesn't expire in v1.
10. **PECR:** Mark's position is that a LinkedIn message doesn't count as electronic mail, so the company-only prospect rule (decision 11) is for email campaigns only.
11. **Build order:** each phase from 3 to 6 covers both providers. The HeyReach half can be tested first, since a LinkedIn account needs no warm-up.

### Connection

### Adding prospects (changes to Part 4)

### Sync, replies and events (changes to Parts 5 to 7)

- **Polling is the truth.** Every 5 minutes per connected workspace: campaigns and status, the stats overview by day, campaign leads (invites, acceptances and messages become events), and inbox conversations for campaign prospects. The reply webhook only queues a sync of that conversation (Part 6). No signature is documented, so the URL secret is checked. HeyReach retries 5 times over 24 hours (unconfirmed, test list); polling covers anything lost. The route works like Smartlead's (Part 5).
- **Matching:** prospects and conversations match on the HeyReach lead id once known, then profile. Conversations in a campaign that match no prospect are counted on the campaign page, not dropped silently, and their messages aren't stored.
- **Deduplication:** `dedupe_key` is `heyreach:<campaign>:<lead>:<kind>` for one-off events, and the conversation id and message time for messages.
- **A reply** stores the conversation (decision 4), marks the prospect replied, and notifies the sender's member (and the person who added them, if different). The notification opens the thread.
- **Stopping everything:** a reply marks the person's `queued` and `filling` rows in every other campaign "Skipped: already replied" and sets `stop_requested_at` on `pushing` and `pushed` ones. The sync retries each stop until the provider confirms and then sets `stopped_at`; until then the prospect shows Stopping (stopping one lead in HeyReach is unconfirmed). With the push-time check (Part 4), a reply that lands while a workflow is adding the same person can't slip through.
- **Reply in Orbit** sends through the HeyReach inbox as that sender, is stored as outbound straight away and confirmed on the next sync. Nothing is sent until you press Send.
- **Categories:** HeyReach's auto tag (positive, negative) sets the first category. After that the category lives in Orbit and isn't written back, since nobody works in HeyReach.
- **Actions per prospect:** Remove from campaign (stops the lead, through `stop_requested_at` as above) and Do not contact, with the rights in Part 6. HeyReach pauses whole campaigns, not single people, so Pause and Resume are on the campaign only.
- **Timeline:** invites, acceptances and messages sent show on the contact's timeline from campaign tables, and each reply is an activity (type `linkedin`), as for email (Part 7).
- **Audit, webhooks and triggers:** the same `campaign.*` events carrying `channel`. The `campaign_event` trigger gains Invite accepted and a channel filter (a LinkedIn reply is replied with the channel set to LinkedIn).
